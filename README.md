# Understanding the UK Retail Sector

This project provides an in-depth analysis of historical UK retail sector data spanning from 1989 to 2025. The research aims to identify economic inflection points and analyze cycles of boom and recession to determine the sector's evolutionary patterns. Central to this analysis is the comparison between Sales Volume and Value, a critical distinction used to differentiate real growth from inflationary price hikes that may yield misleading indicators. 
Furthermore, the project evaluates the resilience of Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) compared to large-scale corporations during economic crises. Finally, the study employs Seasonal Adjustment techniques to eliminate transient factors, uncovering the true underlying performance trends across the years and months.

# Methodology
Data Source: www.ons.gov.uk 
Size and Format: The main data set is 10 MB before ETL, which becomes two data sets with an average of 450 rows and 36 columns to start the analysis process.  
Tools and Technologies:
Excel to gather the required data and prepare it for cleaning and analysis.
SQL to query data for cleaning and analyze data.
Power BI for reporting and visualizing the data to gain insights.

# Abstract

Do rising sales values reflect a real increase in purchasing power, or are they merely a reflection of price hikes and inflation in the UK market?

# Data-Driven Takeaways

# 1. The Inflationary Gap
With an average sales Value of 3.34 compared to a Volume of 1.92, there is a clear "decoupling" between the two metrics. This suggests that a significant portion of the growth over the decades is Nominal Growth driven by price inflation, rather than a purely organic increase in the quantity of goods sold.

# 2. The 2022 Historic Anomaly
The data shows an unprecedented spike in 2022, where Value reached 565 and Volume hit 512. This represents a historic turning point for UK retail. Unlike other periods, the volume surged almost in tandem with value, indicating a genuine explosion in consumer demand (likely due to pandemic-related shifts and e-commerce acceleration) rather than just price hikes.

# 3. High Correlation & Market Resilience
According to the "Key Influencers" model, a 7.49 unit increase in Value typically drives a 7.12 unit increase in Volume. This strong positive correlation indicates a resilient market; despite inflationary pressures, UK retail spending is still largely translated into actual consumption, avoiding a "Stagflation" trap where value rises while volume collapses.

# 4. Pronounced Seasonality Patterns
The monthly breakdown reveals sharp cyclical fluctuations, with notable peaks in months like April and June and dips in February. This highlights the high "seasonality" of the sector, justifying the need for Seasonal Adjustment techniques to uncover the true underlying performance trend (Trend-Cycle) away from holiday noise.
Conclusion

This analysis confirms that the UK retail sector has undergone radical transformations over the past three decades. While "Sales Value" suggests consistent growth, the "Volume" analysis reveals the true challenge of inflation, which has created a tangible gap between consumer spending and actual consumption. The data identifies 2022 as a historic anomaly that defied traditional market patterns, while seasonality remains the primary driver of monthly fluctuations. 

In conclusion, resilience in this sector depends on distinguishing between nominal and real growth, as well as adapting to the rapid shifts in consumer priorities and economic cycles.
